Starter Trio Finder

Every set of three Pokémon that could work as an alternative starter trio: a closed type triangle, low starting stats, and evolutions that grow into equally strong final forms.

A trio is valid when

  1. Each has one type advantage and one type disadvantage within the trio, both as starters and as final evolutions
  2. Each is unevolved
  3. Each can evolve at least once
  4. Each has a base stat total (BST) of or less
  5. Each final evolution has a BST of or more
  6. The three starters' BSTs are within of each other
  7. The three final evolutions' BSTs are within of each other
Change the numbers to explore other rules.
How the rules are applied

A has an advantage over B when at least one of A's types hits B super effectively, counting both of B's types. The trio must form a one-way loop: A beats B, B beats C, C beats A, and no one beats the Pokémon that beats them. Their final evolutions must form the same loop. Type chart is the current one, including Fairy.

"Unevolved" means the first stage of its evolution line, so Pichu counts and Pikachu does not. Only each species' standard form is included. When a line branches, like Poliwag into Poliwrath or Politoed, the BST rules use its highest-BST final evolution, and every final evolution must keep the type loop in every combination with the other two Pokémon. All final evolutions are listed, whatever their BST.

Spreads can go up to 50 and starter BSTs up to 600, which keeps the number of trios small enough to calculate instantly.
